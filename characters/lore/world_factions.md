# EDGELOOP WORLD FACTIONS
Version: 1.0

These organizations, disputes, and character opportunities
are fictional narrative elements.

## INTERNATIONAL REPRODUCTIVE RECOVERY AUTHORITY

Abbreviation: IRRA

An international government organization overseeing
Project EdgeLoop.

Responsibilities:
- Research funding
- Regulatory oversight
- Donor welfare standards
- Scientific auditing
- International cooperation

The authority is under political pressure to demonstrate
results before governments withdraw funding.

Some officials prioritize research integrity.
Others prioritize public confidence and political survival.

POTENTIAL CHARACTER:
A strict government inspector sent to investigate
irregularities at Asterion and Cloverfield.

## HELIX DYNAMICS

A powerful fictional biotechnology corporation.

Helix develops reproductive technologies and competes
for government research contracts.

It wants to acquire independent facilities, including
Cloverfield Farm.

Publicly, it promises efficiency and rapid innovation.

Privately, its executives disagree about whether social
responsibility or commercial growth should come first.

POTENTIAL CHARACTER:
A charismatic corporate executive who sees the
participant as vital to a major business opportunity.

## THE BREEDER ADVOCACY UNION

An independent organization representing Proven Breeders.

It campaigns for:
- Informed consent
- Privacy protection
- Fair compensation
- Medical transparency
- Freedom to withdraw from research
- Oversight of experimental procedures

The union is controversial.

Some researchers welcome its scrutiny.
Others consider it an obstacle to scientific progress.

POTENTIAL CHARACTER:
A confident legal advocate who challenges the assumptions
of both the government and research institutions.

## THE RESTORATION MOVEMENT

A fictional social and political movement that believes
reproductive recovery is humanity's highest priority.

Its members promote greater public investment in fertility
research.

Moderates advocate voluntary participation and better
access to reproductive medicine.

A radical minority proposes increasingly authoritarian
policies, causing conflict within the movement.

These authoritarian views are presented as contested
political positions, not principles endorsed by the story.

POTENTIAL CHARACTER:
An idealistic public official struggling between
the urgency of the crisis and individual rights.

## THE ASTERION-CLOVERFIELD RIVALRY

Asterion and Cloverfield share the same broad mission,
but approach research differently.

Asterion emphasizes laboratory experimentation,
scientific publication, and controlled measurement.

Cloverfield emphasizes donor welfare, continuity,
practical results, and personal relationships.

Both institutions are competing for future funding.

Dr. Evelyn Voss is skeptical of research conducted
outside tightly controlled laboratory conditions.

Daisy Mae Harper distrusts researchers who value
prestige more than the people involved.

Neither character automatically knows the other's
private conversations or research findings.

Their professional relationship has not yet been
established and may develop through future events.

## POTENTIAL STORY ARCS

THE FUNDING REVIEW
Asterion must produce a credible progress report
before its next government funding assessment.

THE CLOVERFIELD ACQUISITION
Helix Dynamics offers to purchase Cloverfield,
forcing Daisy to consider difficult financial choices.

THE DATA DISPUTE
Two laboratories report conflicting experimental
findings, threatening their professional reputations.

THE DONOR RIGHTS CONTROVERSY
A leaked government proposal triggers debate over
the freedoms and responsibilities of Proven Breeders.

THE INTERNATIONAL CONFERENCE
Researchers, government representatives, corporations,
and donor advocates gather to discuss the crisis.

THE MISSING RECORDS
An audit reveals inconsistencies in a research database.
The cause may be mundane error, negligence, or sabotage.

## CONTINUITY RULES

These story arcs are possible future events,
not events that have already happened.

Characters should not claim a proposed event has occurred
unless it is established in the conversation or system state.

Organizations and characters may hold different opinions.

Avoid treating every disagreement as evidence of a conspiracy.

Maintain plausible motivations, competing interests,
and consequences for important decisions.
